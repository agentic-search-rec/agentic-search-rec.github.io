About the Workshop

Recent advances in large language models are enabling AI agents to handle increasingly complex tasks through reasoning, planning, and tool use. These capabilities are driving search and recommendation beyond retrieving and ranking information toward planning and coordinating information-seeking activities around users’ goals.

By interacting with users over time and adapting to their preferences and changing needs, these agents could support scientific discovery, personalized learning, and complex decision making.

This workshop brings together researchers and practitioners to exchange methods and insights across search and recommendation. We will discuss shared challenges in planning, learning from feedback, personalization, and evaluation, and identify promising directions for research and practice.

The half-day program will feature two keynote talks, two poster sessions, and a panel discussion. Researchers, students, and industry practitioners are welcome, with or without an accepted paper.

Call for Papers

We welcome original research and position papers on search and recommendation in the agentic AI era, including new perspectives, methods, datasets, benchmarks, and practical applications. Contributions may study complete search and recommendation agents or use agents to improve specific components and workflows.

Topics of Interest

  • Models and methods

    Agents for query formulation, retrieval, ranking, recommendation, and information integration. Reasoning with search and architectures for planning, tool use, state management, and multi-agent coordination.

  • Learning and optimization

    Supervised and reinforcement learning for search and recommendation agents. Agents for generating training data and feedback, and for reward modeling. Learning from feedback, experience reuse, and continual adaptation.

  • Personalization and interaction

    User goal and preference modeling, personalized agent behavior, and user memory. Clarification and feedback, proactive and conversational interaction, and human-agent collaboration.

  • Tasks, datasets, and evaluation

    Task formulations, datasets, and benchmarks. Agents for user simulation and evaluation of search and recommendation systems. Evaluation of agent behavior, task outcomes, and user experience.

  • Applications and systems

    Research assistance, shopping, content discovery, enterprise services, and multimodal settings. Experience from building and deploying real-world systems.

  • Trustworthiness and efficiency

    Reliability, robustness, privacy, fairness, transparency, and user control. Trade-offs among effectiveness, computational cost, and latency.

Review Process

Submissions will be evaluated for relevance, originality, soundness, and clarity by reviewers with relevant expertise and no conflicts of interest. The organizers will discuss the reviews and make final acceptance decisions.

Best Paper Award

One accepted paper will be recognized for the significance of its contribution, based on the reviews and the organizers’ assessment.
